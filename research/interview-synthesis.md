# Interview Synthesis: User Engagement & Retention

**Source:** 3 user interviews — Priya S. (power user, 18mo), Tom R. (churned, left after 5 weeks), Amara L. (new user, 10 days)

## Top 5 Themes

### 1. The app doesn't tell users what to do next
After the initial spending breakdown, users are left without a clear next action. This is the most consistent theme across all three interviews, including the power user's early experience.

> "I have been waiting for Nudge to tell me the next step. I check it occasionally but nothing has changed since the first day." — Amara L.

> "I kept waiting for it to give me something to act on and it never really did." — Tom R.

> "It took about three months to get there and I think most people give up before that." — Priya S.

### 2. The dashboard feels static / unchanging
Multiple users describe the experience as the same every time they open the app, regardless of elapsed time or new activity.

> "The app just showed me the same dashboard every time." — Tom R.

> "I check it occasionally but nothing has changed since the first day." — Amara L.

> "It has become a ritual." — Priya S. *(contrast: for Priya, repetition became a positive ritual once the app started surfacing personalized patterns — see Theme 4)*

### 3. The initial spending breakdown is a genuine "aha moment"
All users — even the churned one — found real value in the first reveal of their spending data.

> "The spending breakdown was shocking in a useful way — I had no idea I was spending that much on subscriptions." — Amara L.

> "I connected three accounts. Set up a budget." — Tom R.

> "I feel like Nudge actually knows my financial habits now." — Priya S. *(reflecting the long-term payoff of that initial data)*

### 4. Personalized pattern recognition is the retention driver — but it takes time to surface
Priya's loyalty is tied specifically to Nudge surfacing a pattern she hadn't consciously noticed. This is the clearest evidence for the "personalization over time" hypothesis — but she explicitly flags the time-to-value problem.

> "The thing that made it stick for me was when I noticed it started surfacing patterns I had not consciously registered — like that I always overspend in the last week of the month." — Priya S.

> "Once I saw that I started planning around it." — Priya S.

> "It took about three months to get there and I think most people give up before that." — Priya S.

### 5. Competing tools win by demanding action, not just showing data
Tom's churn reason is specific: he left for a tool that actively asks something of him, rather than passively displaying information.

> "I switched to YNAB because at least that feels like it is asking something of me." — Tom R.

> "After the initial setup it felt like nothing changed." — Tom R.

> "I really wanted it to work." — Tom R.

## Contradictions & Outliers

- **Ritual vs. stagnation:** Priya frames her weekly Sunday check-in as a positive ritual, while Tom and Amara describe the unchanging dashboard as a reason to disengage. The difference isn't the repetition itself — it's whether the repeated visit surfaces something new each time. For Priya, Nudge eventually started doing that; for Tom and Amara, it never did.
- **Time-to-value gap:** Priya says it took ~3 months for Nudge to start feeling personalized, and explicitly says "most people give up before that." This directly explains Tom's 5-week churn and is a warning sign for Amara at day 10 — both are well inside the window Priya describes as too long to wait.
- **Outlier — Tom's specific competitive benchmark:** Tom is the only one who names a competing product (YNAB) and frames the gap in terms of being "asked" to do something vs. shown something. This reframes the problem not just as "lacking personalization" but possibly "lacking active prompts/tasks," which is a slightly different mechanism than what Priya or Amara describe.

## Implications (flagged as proposed, not yet validated with team)
- The 3-month time-to-value Priya describes is a significant risk: if that's typical, most users will churn before reaching the personalization payoff that makes Nudge sticky.
- "Telling users what to do next" may need to be more directive/active (closer to Tom's "asking something of me") rather than purely insight-surfacing.
