# NPS Feedback Findings — For Marcus

**Source:** 10 raw NPS comments from Nudge users

## Themes Mentioned More Than Once (Ranked by Frequency)

### 1. Repetitive / static experience — 3 mentions
The app shows the same thing every time, with no new information to discover after the first use.

- "After that it just felt repetitive. I already know I spend too much on food."
- "The home feed shows the same things every time I open it. There is nothing new to discover."
- "I wish it would surface things I do not already know about my spending instead of just confirming what I already suspect."

### 2. Lack of actionable guidance — 2 mentions
Users want to be told what to do, not just shown data.

- "I connected my bank, saw my spending was a mess, felt bad, and then just stopped opening the app. Nothing made me want to come back."
- "I want Nudge to feel like a financial coach, not a spending tracker. Right now it just shows me what happened. I need it to tell me what to do."

### 3. Lack of personalization — 2 mentions
Users don't feel the app reflects their individual situation or remembers what they've told it.

- "I set a savings goal but Nudge has never once mentioned it since. It is like it forgot."
- "Deleted after 3 weeks. The app did not feel like it knew me at all. Just generic money tips I could find anywhere."

### 4. Forgettability / no reason to return — 2 mentions
Users disengage not out of dissatisfaction but because the app gives them no prompt to come back.

- "Love the concept. I just forget it exists. If I got one really useful insight a week I would open it every week."
- "The weekly email is the only thing keeping me engaged. The app itself has not given me a reason to open it."

### Single-mention flag (not ranked, but notable)
- **Poorly targeted nudges:** "The nudges feel random. I got a notification that I spent $12 on coffee and I just turned off notifications entirely." Only one mention, but the outcome (notifications disabled entirely) is a high-severity signal worth tracking even at low frequency.

## Praise
- "The first week was genuinely eye-opening." (re: initial spending breakdown)
- "Love the concept."
- "The weekly email is the only thing keeping me engaged." (praise for the email specifically, not the app)

## Complaints
- App becomes repetitive/static after the first use (3 mentions)
- No actionable next step — feels like a tracker, not a coach (2 mentions)
- Doesn't feel personalized; forgets stated goals; generic tips (2 mentions)
- Easy to forget the app exists; no weekly pull-back mechanism (2 mentions)
- Notification triggered by a single small transaction felt random, leading to opt-out (1 mention)
- Initial emotional reaction to spending data was negative with no follow-up support (counted in actionable guidance, above)

## Top 3 Actionable Issues

1. **The app doesn't change after first use.** Highest-frequency complaint (3 mentions). Directly supports the Engage v2 hypothesis that the home feed needs to surface new, personalized content over time rather than a static view.
2. **Users want direction, not just data.** Multiple users explicitly ask for "what to do" rather than "what happened" — this points toward the proposed weekly summary screen needing a clear, single actionable nudge, not just a recap.
3. **Personalization gaps undermine trust.** A stated savings goal was never referenced again, and nudges were perceived as random rather than relevant — both suggest the product isn't using the data it already has. Fixing this (e.g., goal progress in the weekly summary, smarter nudge targeting) is lower-lift than building new insight-ranking logic and could be a quick win.

## Note on scope
This is feedback from 10 NPS comments — useful for directional signal, not statistically representative. Worth cross-referencing against the 3-user interview synthesis (`research/interview-synthesis.md`), which independently surfaced the same top theme (static/repetitive experience) and the same time-to-value concern.
